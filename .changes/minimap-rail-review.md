`Minimap` and the `VerticalForm` rail, reviewed. A rail that scrolls (`minMarkerSize`) now moves in step with the content, like an editor minimap: the band travels the visible rail from top to bottom as the content goes from start to end, and a drag moves it 1:1 over that whole range (the rail used to follow only when the band reached an edge, and a drag had to rest against the edge to reach the rest). The rail, its markers and its band are measured before the first paint, so the content no longer paints at full width and then reflows as the rail appears. A heading label ends in an ellipsis inside the rail (it was cut by the rail's edge), and `--sf-minimap-width` is 4 units (3 units held less text once the unit shrank to 20px). `VerticalForm`'s `minBlock` defaults to `0.75`, the height of a rail label, so a dense form keeps every field's name (every second one was dropped) and the active name stays inside the viewport band. Scrolling costs less: one rail map per geometry instead of one per frame, no forced layout, and no re-render per percent scrolled. A drag ends when its pointer capture is lost (`usePointerDrag`).
