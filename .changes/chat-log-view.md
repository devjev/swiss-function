Chat: a log view (`variant="log"`) for a panel, with no line spent on spacing. No blank lines between entries or the parts of a reply and no bands: the user's line starts with a `>` and is set in the accent (`--sf-chat-user-fg`), the reply in ink, the reply is indented under it, and a tool call is one line with its status at the end, its output folded to one line under an elbow (`└ last line +11 lines`) until a press or Ctrl+O opens it. The chat drops its frame in this view (`frame` brings it back). `ChatDrawer variant="log"` fills the panel with it: no gutter around the chat, no frame or cast on it, and a rule under the header. Code in a reply shows as code in the log view: highlighted, one cell in, without the fence lines, with a blank line after it. Markdown's terminal reading highlights a fenced block whose language it knows (JavaScript and TypeScript, Rust, Go, the C family, Python, Ruby, shell, SQL, JSON, YAML, CSS, and a diff by line) in CodeEditor's restrained `primary` style: keywords in the accent, names bold, strings italic, comments a step back. A small built-in tokenizer does it, with no grammar and no new dependency; a fence with no language stays plain. `--sf-prose-code-fence: none` drops the fence lines, `--sf-prose-code-indent` sets the code's indent and `--sf-prose-code-after` the space after it. The terminal reading also lets a table wider than the text scroll inside itself; it was cut at the chat's edge on a phone. Refs #111.
